# Quiz — Chapter 6: Brian Kernighan

*About 20 minutes. Multiple choice: 2 points each. Short answer: 5 points each. Short essay: 10 points. Total: 34 points.*

---

## Part A — Multiple choice

**1.** What made programming really "click" for Kernighan?

- (a) Writing the first C compiler
- (b) Reading *The Mythical Man-Month*
- (c) Interactive computing at MIT's Project MAC in 1966
- (d) Writing COBOL at an internship

**2.** Which parts of *The Practice of Programming* is Kernighan proudest of?

- (a) The chapters on object-oriented design
- (b) The chapters on testing and debugging
- (c) The chapters on performance tuning
- (d) The chapters on C syntax and the standard library

**3.** Kernighan's students tested a program that adds numbers by trying 100 numbers. What would he have them try first?

- (a) No numbers, one number, then two numbers
- (b) A million numbers, to test performance
- (c) Random numbers from a fuzzer
- (d) Negative numbers only

**4.** Kernighan says ALGOL's most important contribution was:

- (a) garbage collection
- (b) block structure, along with recursion and a formal syntax description
- (c) strong static typing
- (d) object orientation

**5.** In Kernighan's account, the waterfall model fits:

- (a) open-source projects with many contributors
- (b) small startups that iterate quickly
- (c) safety-critical systems only
- (d) adversarial relationships where a detailed specification is thrown "over the fence"

**6.** Kernighan's "structured lunch breaks" anecdote illustrates:

- (a) the importance of rest for programmers
- (b) a failed experiment at Bell Labs
- (c) a good idea overextended by promoters before being absorbed into standard practice
- (d) Agile's daily stand-up meetings

**7.** Which pair of people does Kernighan hold up as heroes for bringing theory into practice and for good taste?

- (a) Fred Brooks and Bjarne Stroustrup
- (b) Dijkstra and Knuth
- (c) Alan Kay and Barbara Liskov
- (d) Al Aho and Ken Thompson

---

## Part B — Short answer (3–5 sentences each)

**8.** Kernighan says "it's hard to get the program to do what you want... And sometimes what you thought you wanted to do turns out not to be what you really wanted, either." Explain how this sentence contains both *verification* and *validation*.

**9.** Explain why C++'s first implementation as a translator to C was a pragmatic decision. Name one benefit and one long-term cost Kernighan mentions.

---

## Part C — Short essay (1–2 paragraphs)

**10.** Kernighan describes his own approach to programs as "immediate gratification... the antithesis of design," but designs his *books* top-down. Using Brooks's "plan to throw one away" and the sunk-cost problem he describes, explain the risks of the cut-and-try approach. Then argue whether, for a semester-long team project, you would require a throwaway prototype. Justify your answer.
